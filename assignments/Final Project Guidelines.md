### General info:

Final Project is the exam for this class (there won't be a separate exam meeting!), it is worth 40% of the final grade.

Final project as a homework that you design for yourself. Ideally, your project should help you with your linguistic research outside of the course.

### Some suggestions:

* a project that analyzes fieldwork data (db, dataframes, parsing, visualization)
* a project that analyzes literary data (performs NER on novels, produces graphs of characters and the relations between them, uses BERT and clusterization/classification methods to classify or clusterize characters or their speech, produces a morphological or syntactic profile of a particular author or character)
* a project that builds upon the practice project that we have worked on on October 10th (transform the data from your graph into a typological matrix where the columns will represent lemmas and rows will represent synsets; reduce the dimensionality of the matrix to two dimensions, e.g. using PCA or MDS, visualize the result; analyze the result and compare it to the graph from our practice session; manually select one word for each synset, take word2vec vectors for these words from an existing word2vec model, construct a matrix, reduce its dimensionality to two dimensions, visualize, compare the results to the results of the typological matrix; comment upon the results)

### Grading:

8 points -- your work reads as a mini-research paper with internal logic, hypotheses, comments, conclusions, the code is clear, concise, the project shows complexity by using a variety of tools from different areas; in-class presentation has excellent timing, is engaging and states the goals and the results of the project clearly, without the in-class presentation an excellent project will receive max. 7 points. 

9, 10 points -- bonus points for the most impressive (complex and original) projects; you will have the opportunity to contribute to the selection, so please attend all the presentations on October 24th.
